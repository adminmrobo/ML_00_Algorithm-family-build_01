The House Manual — How We Train and Evaluate

Across the fifteen districts of the main series, the Assistant always announced numbers: validation accuracy, overlap with the answers, cumulative reward. This appendix is a manual that gathers in one place what data and what procedure produced those numbers. Whenever you are reading an episode and wonder, "Can I trust this number?", come here.

The three Houses learn from different materials, so they measure differently. The Supervised House compares against the answers of unseen data, the Unsupervised House measures the quality of the structure it found, and the Reinforcement House measures the reward earned in an evaluation environment. Yet one rule is the same for all three: never give the final score using the information you used to choose the model.

02
Supervised: splitting

The Supervised House — Each Share Should Resemble the Whole

Split at random, and a rare grade can vanish from the test set

Story

A factory has 200 products, each with a quality grade: OK 70%, hold 20%, defective 10%. We set aside 20% of them (40 products) as the test set.

If we just shuffle and draw 40, luck may give us only one or two defectives — or as many as 9 (the true share would be 4). If the test set has too few defectives, we can’t properly measure "does it catch defectives well?"

Stratified sampling (stratify) draws 20% from each grade separately and combines them. Then the grade ratios in training and test match the whole. Click the two methods on the right to compare.

Note: a stratified split matches only the ratio of answer grades. It does not make the distribution of other features the same.

Split it yourself · 40 of 200 to the test set
Data that flows in time

Data with an order — sales, stock prices, temperatures — is not shuffled. Learn from the past, measure on the future. Shuffle before splitting and the model ends up predicting "today" having already seen "tomorrow". The 4-11 baseline "tomorrow will be the same as today" is the first opponent in this case.

Data from the same person or machine

Several photos of one patient, or many rows of records from one machine, go entirely to one side. If different photos of the same person are split between training and test, the model gets them right by recognizing the person, not the disease. This is called group leakage (scikit-learn’s GroupKFold).

Manual ②Split skewed grades with stratification, time-ordered data in time order, and keep each subject on one side.
03
Cross-validation

Cross-Validation — Don’t Bet on the Luck of One Split

Rotate the validation share, measure five times, and look at the mean and the wobble together

Story

We have 200 points divided by a wavy boundary (10% of the answers were flipped on purpose). The model is a depth-3 decision tree.

Split just once into 80% training · 20% validation, and the score changes a lot depending on which points landed in validation. The gray dots below are the validation accuracies from thirty separate splits.

5-fold cross-validation divides the data into five shares, then takes turns using one share for validation and learning from the other four. The mean of the five scores is the skill; the standard deviation is the wobble. Every point is used for validation exactly once.

Stratified cross-validation (StratifiedKFold) matches the answer ratio in each fold. For classification problems, this is the default.

Animation · 5-fold cross-validation

Recommended procedure — the vault opens once, at the end

① All the dataFirst set aside the test set and seal it in the vault
→
② Remaining training dataChoose the model and settings (depth, k, regularization…) with cross-validation
→
③ Retrain the chosen modelon all the training data
→
④ One final testOpen the vault and look at the score; make no changes after that
Preprocessing goes inside the fold too

Filling missing values, standardizing, and selecting features are also "learning" steps. In each round of cross-validation they must learn from the training share only. Standardize with all the data and then split, and information from the validation share leaks in. Put preprocessing and the model together in a scikit-learn Pipeline, and each fold automatically learns only from its training share. How much this leak actually inflates the score is measured directly in 4-11 · 04 Finding leaks.

Nested cross-validation

If you try twenty settings with cross-validation and pick the best, even that top score is slightly inflated by luck. When you need to be strict, as in research, use nested cross-validation (nested CV): an outer cross-validation measures the score, and an inner one chooses the settings. With plenty of data, the sealed test set from ① plays the same role.

Manual ③Report a score with both its mean and its wobble. Learn even preprocessing inside the fold. Open the test set only once, after choosing is done.
04
Unsupervised: judging structure

The Unsupervised House — Do You Have to Split?

With no answers, measure how tight each group is and how far apart the groups are

When you needn’t split, and when you must

If the goal is to see the structure of the data you have now (e.g. how many groups do our customers form?), you may run clustering on all the data and measure group quality with metrics. There are no "unseen answers" to predict.

If it’s a model to use on data that will come in later (e.g. assigning new customers to existing groups), set part of the data aside, assign it to the groups built from the training data, and check that the quality holds. This is a different check from classification accuracy, though.

When hidden answers exist separately (e.g. actual customer grades confirmed later), you can measure how well the clusters agree with them using ARI · NMI. If there are answers for evaluation, as in anomaly detection, precision and recall work too.

05
Reinforcement: splitting worlds

The Reinforcement House — Split Environments, Not Data

Doing well in the room you learned in is not the same as doing well in a room you’ve never seen

Story

Reinforcement learning has no data table collected in advance. The agent creates its own experience by moving around in the environment. So instead of "split 80 : 20", we split the environment it learns in and the conditions it is evaluated under.

Sweepy the cleaning bot learned to find its charger with Q-learning over 400 runs in the training room (6×6, six wall cells). Then learning stops (frozen policy) and we measure in two ways.

① Same room, different starting points: other episodes of the same environment. ② A room never seen before with a different wall layout: the environment itself has changed. If you update the Q table during evaluation, that’s not evaluation — it’s more training.

In a game, ② would be a new map; for a robot, new objects or new lighting. In any field, skill shows only when measured under "conditions not used for learning".

Measure it yourself · one policy, two rooms
Manual ⑤In reinforcement learning, freeze the policy during evaluation and measure other episodes of the same environment separately from unseen environments. Report the mean and wobble over several seeds. Measure the real goal separately from the reward (4-14 The Reinforcement House’s Ledger).
06
Depth and overfitting

Depth and Overfitting at a Glance

Get greedy for training score, and you collapse on unseen data

The trap every House shares is overfitting. For a decision tree, the knob is depth (how many yes/no questions it may ask in a row). As depth grows, the training score heads to 100%, but from some point on the score on unseen data falls. The cross-validation of section 03 above is exactly the tool for choosing "at what depth to stop".

What depth is, and what happens as the questions grow to 2, 3, 4 levels, is shown in pictures in 4-2 The Supervised House · What is depth?. For other models the knobs are KNN’s k, SVM’s C · γ, and logistic regression’s regularization strength (4-7 The Classification Eight).

07
The one rule

The Bottom Line — Three Houses, One Rule

They measure different things, but the vault opens the same way

Supervised House

How well does it predict the answers of unseen data? Match ratios with a stratified split, choose with cross-validation, and measure once on the sealed test set.

→ 4-12 The Supervised House’s Ledger

Unsupervised House

How good is the structure it found? Analyzing all the data is fine, but a model for new data needs a separate check.

→ 4-13 The Unsupervised House’s Ledger

Reinforcement House

How much reward does the frozen policy earn under evaluation conditions? Separating learning from evaluation stages and environments matters more than data ratios.

→ 4-14 The Reinforcement House’s Ledger

One-sentence summary

You can trust performance only when the information used to choose the model is kept separate from the information used for the final evaluation.
